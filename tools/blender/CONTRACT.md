# Asset contract (Blender -> GLB -> game)

Every model in the game is generated by a Blender Python script in this folder, so it can be rebuilt and
restyled at any time. This file is the contract between those scripts and the engine loader.

## Tooling
- Blender 4.2 LTS, headless: `blender -b -P tools/blender/<family>.py`
  (Linux build: https://download.blender.org/release/Blender4.2/blender-4.2.9-linux-x64.tar.xz).
- `tools/blender/lib.py` holds the shared helpers (materials, shading, export, manifest, preview renders).
  Import it; do not edit it. Put family-specific helpers in your own script.

## Files per family
- `tools/blender/<family>.py`                  the generator (deterministic, no manual steps)
- `public/assets/models/<family>.glb`          one GLB holding every asset of the family
- `public/assets/models/<family>.json`         manifest written by `lib.export()`
- `tools/blender/previews/<family>_*.jpg`      preview contact sheets written by `lib.preview()`
Touch nothing else in the repository.

## Scale and axes
- Metres, real-world sizes (door 2.05 x 0.9, sedan about 4.9 x 1.85 x 1.4, adult 1.75 tall, kerb 0.15).
- Blender Z is up. Origin of every asset = centre of its footprint on the ground (z = 0 is the floor it stands on).
- Props, furniture, plants, people, structures: the FRONT faces Blender -Y (it becomes +Z in the game).
- Vehicles: forward is Blender +X, left is +Y.
- One top-level mesh object per asset, named `lower_snake_case`, unique across the family.
  Parts the engine must move are separate child objects named `<asset>.<part>` with their origin on the pivot
  (wheels, steering wheel, door, sign blade, limbs). Everything else is joined into the asset mesh.

## Materials (this is how the engine shades and recolours)
Name every material `<key>` or `<key>__<label>`; set Principled Base Color to the final colour.
Keys the engine knows:
- `paint`  matte surfaces (walls, plastic, cloth, wood, skin)
- `gloss`  car paint and enamel
- `metal`  bare or galvanised metal      - `chrome`  bright chrome
- `rubber` tyres, mats                   - `glass`   dark reflective glass you cannot see through
- `clear`  see-through glazing           - `light`   glowing parts (lamps, neon, lit signs)
- `atlas`  printed graphics: ONE material per family may carry an embedded base-colour texture (<= 1024 px)
  with UVs, for decals, labels, sign faces, dashboards. Everything else is flat colour without UVs.
Labels mark parts the engine recolours per instance, for example `gloss__body`, `gloss__body2` (two-tone),
`paint__skin`, `paint__hair`, `paint__shirt`, `paint__pants`, `paint__shoes`, `paint__fabric`, `paint__wood`.
Use as many distinct flat colours as the model needs; each distinct colour is its own material.

## Shape language (what "matches the references" means)
- Mid-poly: clearly more refined than voxel/box art, clearly not high-poly. Flat colours carry the look.
- Hard-surface forms get real chamfers (bevel 1-3 segments) on the edges that catch light; curved parts use
  10-16 sides. Smooth shading with sharp edges above 35 degrees (`lib.finish()` does this).
- Model the parts that read from 3-10 m away as geometry, not as colour: frames, handles, hinges, grilles,
  bumpers, trims, cushions, shelves with goods, buttons, lips and gaps.
- Nothing floats, nothing interpenetrates visibly, no inverted normals, no z-fighting coplanar faces
  (offset overlays by >= 3 mm).
- Budgets (triangles): small prop 100-1500, furniture 200-3000, vehicle body 6000-14000 including the cabin,
  wheel 500-1200, person 1500-3500, tree 1500-6000. Stay inside unless the asset is a landmark.
- Original designs only: no brand names, logos or text copied from the reference screenshots.

## Review loop (required)
Render previews with `lib.preview()`, look at them next to the reference sheets, fix what looks cruder than the
reference, and repeat until a side-by-side reads as the same art style. Commit the final preview sheets.
